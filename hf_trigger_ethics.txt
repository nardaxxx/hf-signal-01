HF_SIGNAL_01 — RECOGNITION AND RESPONSIBILITY

Attacking a person who is hors de combat is prohibited under customary
international humanitarian law — recorded as Rule 47 of the ICRC Customary
IHL Study, historically rooted in Article 23(c) of the Hague Regulations, and
stated in treaty terms for States party to Additional Protocol I in Article 41.
The prohibition binds every party to an armed conflict, whatever it has
ratified.

HF SIGNAL 01 does not create that obligation, and the obligation does not
depend on its adoption. The signal is a publicly documented form in which an
expression of surrender can be made machine-readable. Its existence removes
categorical technical impossibility as a ground for not recognising surrender,
and turns that ground into a documented question of design, review and
validation.

WHAT THE SIGNAL DOES NOT DO

It confers no status. A person is protected because of their condition, not
because of any signal they emit.

It is a supplementary means of expressing an intention to surrender, never a
condition of protection. Its absence permits no adverse inference as to
protection or targetability.

It does not displace the ordinary manifestations of surrender — raised hands,
laid-down weapons, a white cloth — which remain what they have always been.

Civilian protection does not depend on surrender or on machine-readable
signalling. Civilians are protected against direct attack independently of any
expression on their part.

STATUS

Candidate specification. Technology readiness level 2: no validated detector,
no annotated dataset, no measured latency, no independent validation. The
specification is offered to be tested, amended or superseded.

Where a system cannot register a legally protected condition, that is a
determination made at design — and one that can be examined.
